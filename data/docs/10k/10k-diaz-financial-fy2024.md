# Diaz Financial - Form 10-K Extract (Fiscal 2024)

## Item 1. Business
Diaz Financial is a financial company operating through three reportable segments: Payments,
Asset Management and Consumer Lending. Our principal executive offices are located in Boston, Massachusetts. As of the end of fiscal 2024, we employed approximately 56,410 people worldwide. We sell to customers in more
than 72 countries and our strategy emphasises disciplined capital
allocation, operational excellence and organic growth.

## Item 1A. Risk Factors
- Supply-chain disruptions may delay product shipments and increase input costs. We monitor this exposure through our enterprise risk management framework and report it to the Audit Committee each quarter. The occurrence of any of these events could have a material adverse effect on our business, financial condition and results of operations.
- A downturn in global economic conditions could reduce demand for our products. Insurance coverage may not be sufficient to cover all losses arising from these risks. The occurrence of any of these events could have a material adverse effect on our business, financial condition and results of operations.
- Acquisitions may not deliver the expected synergies and could divert management attention. Regulators in several jurisdictions have increased their scrutiny of this area, which could raise our compliance costs. We have experienced such events in the past and expect to continue to face them.
- We depend on third-party cloud providers; a prolonged outage could disrupt operations and damage customer relationships. Insurance coverage may not be sufficient to cover all losses arising from these risks. Although we maintain mitigation plans, there can be no assurance that these measures will be effective in all circumstances.
- Intense competition could reduce our market share or force us to lower prices. Regulators in several jurisdictions have increased their scrutiny of this area, which could raise our compliance costs. The occurrence of any of these events could have a material adverse effect on our business, financial condition and results of operations.
- We may be unable to attract and retain the highly skilled employees our strategy needs. Regulators in several jurisdictions have increased their scrutiny of this area, which could raise our compliance costs. Although we maintain mitigation plans, there can be no assurance that these measures will be effective in all circumstances.
- Litigation and regulatory investigations could result in significant fines or restrictions on our business. Regulators in several jurisdictions have increased their scrutiny of this area, which could raise our compliance costs. Although we maintain mitigation plans, there can be no assurance that these measures will be effective in all circumstances.

## Item 2. Properties
We own or lease manufacturing, distribution, research and office facilities in North America, Europe and Asia. We believe our facilities are suitable and adequate for our current needs and that additional space can be obtained on commercially reasonable terms if required.

## Item 3. Legal Proceedings
From time to time we are party to litigation, claims and regulatory proceedings arising in the ordinary course of business. While the outcome of these matters is inherently uncertain, management does not currently expect them to have a material adverse effect on our consolidated financial position.

## Item 7A. Quantitative and Qualitative Disclosures About Market Risk
We are exposed to market risks from changes in interest rates, foreign-currency exchange rates and commodity prices. We use derivative instruments, principally interest-rate swaps and foreign-currency forwards, to manage these exposures, and we do not enter into derivatives for speculative purposes. A hypothetical 100 basis point increase in interest rates would not have a material effect on our interest expense given the predominantly fixed-rate nature of our borrowings.

## Item 7. Management's Discussion and Analysis
Total revenue for fiscal 2024 was $43,302 million, an increase of 9.7 percent compared with the prior year. Operating margin was 20.4 percent and net income was
$6,785 million. The Payments segment was our largest, contributing $18,826 million of revenue.

Segment revenue:
- Payments: $18,826 million
- Asset Management: $16,066 million
- Consumer Lending: $8,409 million

Results reflected volume growth partly offset by
higher input costs.

## Liquidity and Capital Resources
Long-term debt totalled $24,679 million, and cash and cash equivalents were $12,646 million. Capital expenditures were $4,098 million, primarily for capacity expansion and technology infrastructure. We did not pay a dividend during the year and prioritised debt reduction. We believe
existing cash, operating cash flow and our revolving credit facility will be sufficient to
fund operations for at least the next twelve months.
